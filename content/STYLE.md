# Mizaj content style guide

Every piece of copy on mizaj.pk, in the paid reports and on our socials, follows this guide.

## Who we write for
Pakistanis aged 16–35, urban and semi-urban, comfortable reading English, plus the diaspora (UK, Gulf, US, Canada). They are smart, busy, on a phone, and used to generic "you are a natural leader" filler. Beat that by being specific.

## Voice
- Warm, witty, insightful, never preachy. Like a wise elder cousin who also reads psychology.
- Second person ("you"). Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences). Concrete examples over abstractions.
- Pakistani life shows up naturally, not in every line: family WhatsApp groups, shaadi season, chai, cricket, load-shedding, university and entry tests, parents' expectations, joint family life, rishta conversations, freelancing, CSS exams, office politics, Ramadan, Eid, traffic, mehndi dances, cousins.
- Light Roman Urdu is welcome (yaar, bas, jugaar, mehfil, sabr, dil, izzat), at most one or two touches per section.
- Gender-neutral wherever possible.

## Hard rules
1. Never use the words "MBTI", "Myers-Briggs", "16Personalities" or their type names (Architect, Logician, Advocate, Mediator and so on). We say "your type" or "the 16 Mizaj types", and we use our own names (below).
2. No clinical or medical claims. Personality content is self-reflection, not diagnosis. Temperament (Unani) content is traditional wellness wisdom: say "traditionally believed", "many people find", "Unani tradition suggests". Never say a food cures, treats or prevents a disease. Recommend a qualified doctor or hakeem for any health concern.
3. Astrology, numerology and palm reading are labelled entertainment: "for fun", "a mirror, not a prophecy". Never predict deaths, illness, divorce, money windfalls or specific future events.
4. Respect Islamic values and Pakistani sensibilities: relationships are framed as marriage, rishta, friendship and family (no dating-app or nightlife framing, no alcohol). Never mock any ethnicity, province, sect, religion, class or gender.
5. Do not name real living people as examples of a type. Use fictional characters (global films, books, cartoons and well-known Pakistani TV drama archetypes described generically) or relatable archetypes ("the cousin who plans every family trip").
6. Original writing only. Do not paraphrase any existing personality website.
