# Mizaj content style guide (v2: desis everywhere)

Every word on mizaj.pk, in the paid reports and on our socials follows this guide. v2 (28 Oct 2026) moves Mizaj from
a Pakistan-only brand to an international one for South Asians everywhere.

## Who we write for
Desis worldwide, aged 18–35: Pakistani, Indian, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan and Nepali, living in London, Birmingham,
Toronto, New York, Houston, Dubai, Riyadh, Sydney, Karachi, Lahore, Mumbai, Delhi or Dhaka. Many grew up between two
cultures. They read English first, know everyday Hindi-Urdu words, and are tired of personality content that either
ignores their family life or turns it into a stereotype. Write for someone who could be in Toronto or Karachi.

## Voice
- International first, desi as the seasoning: smart, warm, witty and internet-native, like a brilliant desi friend who
  reads psychology. Premium, never folksy.
- Second person ("you"). Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences). Specific observations over generic praise.
- One or two desi touches per section, never every line. Shared, cross-border references only:
  the family group chat, aunties with opinions, rishta pressure, wedding functions and the dance practice before them,
  chai, cricket, biryani debates, desi dramas and films, "log kya kahenge", the doctor-engineer-lawyer conversation,
  being the eldest daughter or son, big extended families, moving abroad or visiting home, first-gen university life,
  corporate jobs and side hustles, festival holidays.
- Everyday words that cross borders are welcome: yaar, chai, aunty, beta, jugaad, nazar, scene, timepass, sabr, dil.
  At most one or two per section; never explain them condescendingly.
- Gender-neutral wherever possible.

## Avoid (too local or divisive)
- Country-specific institutions and systems: CSS, MDCAT, ECAT, NUST, FSc, matric boards, BC committees, load-shedding,
  PKR amounts in prose, specific Pakistani or Indian cities as the default setting (mix cities when you name any).
- Politics, India–Pakistan rivalry, nationalism, flags, religion-specific practice as a default. Faith-inclusive:
  "wedding" or "marriage" (not "nikah" unless the context is clearly Muslim), "fasting days" (Ramadan, vrat or any
  fast), "festivals" or "Eid or Diwali" only when both genuinely fit.
- Caste, sect, colourism, regional stereotypes, mocking any community.
- Village or folkloric scenes as the default picture of desi life. Our people are modern, urban and global.

## Hard rules (unchanged)
1. Never use the words "MBTI", "Myers-Briggs", "16Personalities" or their type names (Architect, Logician,
   Commander, Advocate, Mediator and so on). We say "your type" or "the 16 Mizaj types" and use our own names.
2. No clinical or medical claims. Personality content is self-reflection. Temperament content is traditional
   wellness wisdom ("traditionally believed", "many people find"); never say a food cures or treats anything; suggest
   a qualified doctor for health concerns.
3. Astrology, numerology and palm reading are labelled entertainment, a mirror not a prophecy. No predictions.
4. Relationships are framed as friendship, family, relationships and marriage; no hook-up or dating-app framing.
5. No real living people as examples. Fictional characters or relatable archetypes only.
6. Original writing only.

## The four mizaj (temperaments)
Primary label is the element; the Unani name is second; the classical Greek name is context.

| Key | Primary | Unani name | Quality | Classical | Nickname |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| damvi | Air | Damvi | warm and moist | Sanguine | The Breeze |
| safravi | Fire | Safravi | warm and dry | Choleric | The Flame |
| balghami | Water | Balghami | cool and moist | Phlegmatic | The River |
| saudavi | Earth | Saudavi | cool and dry | Melancholic | The Mountain |

A Mizaj profile combines both: "INFJ · Earth". "Garam ya thanda?" (warm or cool?) stays as our playful hook.

Brand story: the four temperaments run from Hippocrates and Galen through Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine to modern
temperament psychology. Mizaj ("temperament" in Urdu, Hindi, Persian, Arabic and Turkish) joins that thousand-year
tradition with modern type psychology, for people who carry more than one culture.
