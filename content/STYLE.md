# Mizaj content style guide (v3: for everyone)

Every word on mizaj.pk, in the paid reports, in ads and on our socials follows this guide. v3 (29 Oct 2026, the
owner's decision): Mizaj is for everyone who wants to know more about themselves, the people around them and the
world. It replaces v2 ("desis everywhere"); do not write for one culture or region.

## Who we write for
Curious people of every age and background, anywhere:
- **Students** choosing subjects, a degree or a first job.
- **Professionals** figuring out how they work, lead and get along with colleagues.
- **Educators** who want to understand and guide their students.
- **Marketers and managers** who want to understand customers, audiences and teams.
- **Parents, couples, friends and families** who want to understand each other.
- **Young and old:** a 17-year-old and a 70-year-old should both feel the page was written for them.

Write so a reader in London, Lagos, Lahore, Lisbon or Los Angeles nods along, and so a non-native English speaker
reads it easily.

## Voice
- Smart, warm, witty and clear: a well-read friend who knows psychology and history. Premium, never folksy, never
  clinical.
- Second person ("you"). Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences). Plain words; explain any term once. Specific observations
  over generic praise.
- Situations everyone knows: school and university, the first job and the tenth, meetings and deadlines, group chats,
  family dinners and holidays, weddings and birthdays, moving house or country, travel, cooking, sport, hobbies,
  raising children, retirement, friendship, caring for parents.
- When a detail needs a place, food, festival or name, vary them across the world, and never let one culture become
  the default.
- Mix ages, life stages and jobs across examples: a student, a nurse, a teacher, a founder, a grandparent.
- Gender-neutral wherever possible.

## Avoid
- In-jokes or slang that only one country or community gets (including Hindi-Urdu words such as yaar, beta, rishta,
  aunty, jugaad), and local institutions (exam boards, national tests, local job systems).
- Politics, nationalism, flags, religion as a default setting, money amounts in a local currency in prose.
- Stereotypes of any nation, community, age group or gender; mocking any group.
- "Young people only" framing. Older readers are part of the audience.

## Hard rules
1. Never use the words "MBTI", "Myers-Briggs", "16Personalities" or their type names (Architect, Logician,
   Commander, Advocate, Mediator and so on). We say "your type" or "the 16 Mizaj types" and use our own names.
2. No clinical or medical claims. Personality content is self-reflection. Temperament content is traditional
   wellness wisdom ("traditionally believed", "many people find"); never say a food cures or treats anything; suggest
   a qualified doctor for health concerns.
3. Astrology, numerology and palm reading are labelled entertainment, a mirror not a prophecy. No predictions, and
   never as a reason to choose or reject a partner.
4. Relationships are framed as friendship, family, couples and marriage; no hook-up or dating-app framing.
5. No real living people as examples. Fictional characters or relatable archetypes only.
6. Original writing only.
7. Phase 1 sells one-time reports only. Don't promise "never a subscription": subscriptions are planned for
   phase 2, and they will be honest (clear price, easy cancel, no hidden renewals).

## The four elements (temperaments)
The element is the name readers see. The classical Greek name and the Unani name are the history behind it.

A Mizaj profile combines both: "INFJ · Earth". The playful hook is "Warm or cool?".

## The story
The four temperaments are one of the oldest ideas about personality: from Hippocrates and Galen to Ibn Sina's
*Canon of Medicine*, which taught them across Europe and Asia for 600 years. Mizaj ("temperament" in Arabic, Persian,
Turkish and Urdu) joins that tradition with modern type psychology: who you are, how you run, and how you fit with
everyone else.
