### Model and the -A/-T trick
- **NERIS Type Explorer® model:** five trait scales. The first four letters give the familiar 16 type codes. The fifth trait, **Identity**, splits every type into **Assertive (-A)** and **Turbulent (-T)** variants, for example INTJ-A and INTJ-T. That makes 32 results. https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai
- **What Identity measures:** how a person reacts to setbacks, feedback and change. Assertive people are described as self-assured and stress-resistant; Turbulent people as success-driven, perfectionistic and self-doubting. https://www.16personalities.com/articles/identity-assertive-vs-turbulent
- **The site's own survey statistic:** 93% of Assertive respondents feel confident facing daily difficulties, against 62% of Turbulent respondents. This comes from the site's own research and has not been independently validated. https://www.16personalities.com/articles/identity-assertive-vs-turbulent
- **No cognitive functions:** the company says it "borrowed the acronym format for convenience" and does not use Jungian cognitive functions. https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai
  - A review describes the result as MBTI-format letters with a "Big-Five-style Identity dimension layered on". https://mypassion.ai/blog/16personalities-career-test-review
- **Why the -A/-T trick works commercially (analysis):**
  1. It makes the model the company's own product (NERIS®), not a copy of MBTI.
  2. It gives 32 variants, which means more content, more comparison articles and more reasons to share ("I'm INFJ-T, you're INFJ-A").
  3. It adds a neuroticism-like scale that has stronger science behind it, which improves credibility.

## 3. Trademark: MBTI and Myers-Briggs

- **Who owns the marks:**
  - The **Myers & Briggs Foundation** owns "Myers-Briggs Type Indicator", "Myers-Briggs", "MBTI", Step I/II/III and the MBTI logo, "in the United States and other countries".
  - **The Myers-Briggs Company** (formerly CPP) has been the exclusive worldwide publisher since 1975.
  - Sources: https://themyersbriggs.com/en-US/Support/Trademarks ; https://www.myersbriggs.org/more-about-personality-type/permissions
- **USPTO records:** MBTI registrations are live and renewed (for example serial 76510940, renewed; and 74377710). https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/76510940/MBTI ; https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/74377710/MBTI
- **Symbols:** MBTI, Myers-Briggs and Myers-Briggs Type Indicator carry ®; Step I–III carry ™. https://www.mbtionline.com/-/media/MBTIonline/Files/Trademark_Guidelines.pdf
- **The owner's usage rules:**
  - Use the mark as an adjective with a noun ("the MBTI® instrument"), never as a noun.
  - Never abbreviate it, parody it, or portray it negatively.
  - Include an acknowledgement statement.
  - Reproducing the instrument requires written permission.
  - Sources: https://shop.themyersbriggs.com/Pdfs/Trademark_Guidelines.pdf ; https://www.myersbriggs.org/more-about-personality-type/permissions
- **Domain names:** the publisher's website guidelines say registering domain names that contain its trademarks "constitutes trademark infringement", so never use domains like "mbti-pakistan.pk". https://www.themyersbriggs.com/en-us/support/website-guidelines
- **Japan:** the Japan MBTI Association publicly warned in July 2023 that 16Personalities is "completely different" from MBTI, and noted that MBTI is a registered trademark in Japan. https://www.j-cast.com/2023/07/03464452.html?p=all
- **How 16Personalities stays clear:**
  1. It uses its own registered brand (16PERSONALITIES®) and its own model name (NERIS Type Explorer®).
  2. It openly says it borrowed only the acronym format and does not use Jungian cognitive functions.
  3. It adds a fifth scale (-A/-T), so its results and type labels differ from MBTI's.
  4. It gives every type a memorable nickname ("Architect", "Advocate", "Logician") and builds its brand on the nicknames rather than on "MBTI".
  5. It writes all of its own type descriptions.
  - Sources: https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai ; https://16personalities.com/academy/adventurer ; https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/97917251/16PERSONALITIES
  - A German commentary describes 16P as legally independent of the licensed MBTI. https://www.profiling-institut.de/persoenlichkeitstest/16personalities-vs-mbti/
- **No lawsuits found:** I found no reported lawsuit or published cease-and-desist letter against 16P or other free "16-type" sites.
- **Are the four-letter codes protected?** I found no ruling either way. The codes (INTJ and so on) appear to be used freely across the industry, but this is my analysis, not legal advice.
- **Pakistan:** **GAP.** I found no record of an MBTI registration in Pakistan. Pakistan can be designated through the WIPO Madrid system, so check the WIPO Global Brand Database and IPO-Pakistan. https://www.examinechina.com/trademark-registration-in-pakistan/
- **Wording that should be safe for a small site (analysis; not legal advice):**
  - Don't use "MBTI" or "Myers-Briggs" in the brand name, domain, logo, app name, or as a product name ("Mizaj MBTI Test").
  - Name the model yourself, for example "Mizaj 16-Type Model".
  - Write your own type nicknames and descriptions. Do not copy text from MBTI or 16P.
  - Refer to MBTI only factually and nominatively, with the ® and a disclaimer. For example: "Mizaj uses four-letter type codes similar in format to those popularised by the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator® (MBTI®). Mizaj is independent and not affiliated with, endorsed by, or licensed by The Myers & Briggs Foundation or The Myers-Briggs Company. MBTI and Myers-Briggs are trademarks of The Myers & Briggs Foundation."
  - Never claim the test is the "official MBTI". Explanatory SEO pages about MBTI are probably fine if they follow the adjective rule ("the MBTI® assessment"), but get a Pakistani IP lawyer's opinion before launch.

## 4. Quiz funnels and web-to-app businesses: benchmarks

*Benchmarks come from vendors and blogs, and each defines its numerator and denominator differently. Treat them as ranges.*

### Quiz start → completion → email capture
- **Completion rate (starters who finish):**
  - A 2026 benchmark blog says typically 35–42%. https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/quiz-funnel-completion-rate-benchmark-2026/
  - Octane AI (a quiz vendor) cites a 60% industry average and 68% for its own quizzes. https://octaneai.com/products/analytics
- **Email capture among completers:**
  - About 58–63% for multiple-choice quizzes with an immediate reward. Personality quizzes with personalised results reach "up to ~70%" on well-built pages [blog claim]. https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/quiz-funnel-completion-rate-benchmark-2026/
  - RevenueHunt says 71% of its high performers make email capture required. https://revenuehunt.com/product-quiz-metrics-what-to-track-to-convert-better/
- **Leads as a share of all visitors (ConvertFlow, Sept 2026, 1,063 campaigns):**
  - Lead-generation quiz funnels: 7.85% of visitors become leads on average.
  - Embedded quizzes: 10.45%.
  - Quiz landing pages: 26.72%.
  - Source: https://www.convertflow.com/benchmark-reports/lead-generation-quiz-funnel-benchmark-september-2026
- **Sales conversion:** quiz traffic converts at about 2% rising to 5% or more, versus the site average [vendor claim]. https://revenuehunt.com/product-quiz-metrics-what-to-track-to-convert-better/

### Paywall, trial and purchase (subscription apps; secondary summaries of RevenueCat and Adapty data)
- **Paywall view → trial start:** about 5–15% (rough rule of thumb). Trial → paid: about 40–70%. https://mwm.ai/glossary/paywall
- **Median trial-to-paid:** about 25.6–27.8% (Adapty data, as cited by blogs). Health and fitness runs higher, at about 35–62% depending on the source. https://pricepush.app/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks ; https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques.md
- **Trial length (RevenueCat via a secondary blog):**
  - Under 4 days: about 25.5%.
  - 5–9 days: about 37.4%.
  - 17–32 days: about 42.5%.
  - Sources: https://pricepush.app/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks ; https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026
- **Paywall placement:** a paywall placed in onboarding converts install-to-paid at 1.78%, against 0.89% for a paywall gated inside the app (Adapty data). https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions

## 7. Credible frameworks a small business can legally use

- **Big Five (IPIP item pool):**
  - **[Unverified in this session]** The International Personality Item Pool (IPIP) states its items are in the public domain and free for any use, including commercial use, without permission. Verify at https://ipip.ori.org/.
  - Evidence of commercial use: JobCannon runs the IPIP-NEO-120 commercially. https://jobcannon.io/assessments/ipip-neo-120/about
  - Research support: the British Psychological Society's Research Digest reported that free personality tests (IPIP-based) are "more reliable and efficient than the paid variety". https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/free-personality-tests-are-more-reliable-and-efficient-paid-variety
  - Oxford's Mind & Brain research group also publishes Big Five material. https://mbrg.bsg.ox.ac.uk/big-five
- **RIASEC / O*NET Interest Profiler:**
  - **[Unverified in this session]** The O*NET Interest Profiler, Holland's six RIASEC interest codes, is produced by the US Department of Labor. O*NET content is released under an open licence (CC BY 4.0) that allows commercial reuse with attribution. Check the current licence terms at https://www.onetcenter.org/ before reuse.
  - RIASEC is the best fit for school and university career guidance.
- **HEXACO:**
  - **[Unverified in this session]** The HEXACO-PI-R items are free for non-commercial research. Commercial use needs the authors' permission (hexaco.org). It adds an Honesty-Humility factor, which is attractive for hiring products.
- **Enneagram:**
  - The nine-type model and the word are widely used, but specific instruments are proprietary; for example, the Enneagram Institute sells the RHETI at $20. https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/?p=1507
  - A small business can write its own Enneagram questionnaire. Do not copy RHETI items.
- **MBTI-style 16 types:**
  - Free to imitate in format, as 16P, Truity's TypeFinder and Boo do.
  - The official MBTI items and marks are copyrighted and trademarked; reproduction needs written permission. https://www.myersbriggs.org/more-about-personality-type/permissions
  - It is the weakest scientifically: reviewers call the type theory "contested in peer-reviewed psychometrics". https://mypassion.ai/blog/16personalities-career-test-review
  - Truity offsets this by publishing a technical manual. https://www.truity.com/sites/default/files/fillpdf/typefinder-technicaldoc.pdf
- **Character strengths (VIA):** the VIA Survey is proprietary but free to take. https://viacharacter.org/survey

## Lessons for mizaj.pk (specific, actionable)

1. **Keep the whole 16-type test and a rich free result free, and win on volume.**
   - Every scaled player (16P with 1.5B+ tests; Truity with about 25M people; VIA) gives the core result away.
   - Paywalling the result itself, as Gallup does, works only for a famous brand with a B2B sales force.

2. **Copy 16P's three-step price ladder, priced for Pakistan (PPP):**
   - **Step 1, low entry:** about $9 globally becomes a small PKR "Deep Report".
   - **Step 2, topic suites:** about $29 globally becomes "Career", "Rishta/Relationships" and "Growth" guides.
   - **Step 3, bundle:** about $99 globally becomes an "All guides + all tests" bundle that anchors the others.
   - Use one-time payments with a 30-day money-back guarantee, which every 16P tier advertises. Avoid the $1-trial-to-subscription trap that generates Trustpilot anger.

3. **Own the IP like NERIS did:**
   - Name your model (for example "Mizaj Type Model").
   - Add a fifth, locally meaningful scale (an -A/-T equivalent) to create 32 variants and new content.
   - Write all type descriptions from scratch, and give types Urdu or English nicknames people will put in bios.

4. **Legal hygiene from day one:**
   - Never use "MBTI" or "Myers-Briggs" in the brand, domain, logo or product names.
   - Use the factual disclaimer drafted in section 3.
   - Get a Pakistani IP lawyer to check WIPO and IPO-Pakistan for MBTI registrations.
   - Register "Mizaj" as your own trademark, as NERIS did with 16PERSONALITIES®.

5. **Localise language aggressively:**
   - 16P runs in 45–49 languages, and its #2 keyword is Japanese ("mbti 診断").
   - Ship Urdu, Roman Urdu and English, and target the search queries "mbti test urdu", "personality test in urdu" and "mizaj test".

6. **Build SEO surfaces that scale:**
   - Type pages; type×type compatibility pages (16×16 = 256); "type + career" and "type + rishta" pages.
   - Pakistan city and university "country profile"-style pages, as 16P does with country profiles.
   - A "/for-ai" reference page so chatbots cite mizaj.pk.

7. **Make compatibility the viral loop:**
   - "Test with your partner, friend or fiancé(e)" with a compatibility percentage (the So Syncd mechanic) and a shareable card.
   - The paid upsell is a "Couple/Rishta Compatibility Report".
   - Lesson from So Syncd: don't build a dating app yourself; it stalled at about 60K users.

8. **Capture email or WhatsApp before showing the full result:**
   - Benchmarks: 35–60%+ of starters complete; 58–70% of completers give contact details when the reward is a personalised result; only about 7.85% of all visitors to a lead-gen quiz funnel become leads (ConvertFlow).
   - Instrument every step and compare against these numbers.

9. **Use BetterMe/Noom-style price anchoring, but honestly:**
   - Show a crossed-out "list" price against an intro price (BetterMe: $38.95 becomes $15.19).
   - Show prices in local currency (Noom).
   - Put the offer right after the result. Onboarding paywalls convert about 2× in-app gates (1.78% vs 0.89%).
   - Show the price before the test starts, to avoid Truity-style "hidden price" complaints.

10. **Plan conservatively on conversion:**
    - Estimates put 16P's premium conversion under 2%.
    - Model 0.5–2% of test-takers buying a one-time report.
    - At 16P-like scale this is a big business; at small scale, B2B must carry revenue.

11. **Launch a per-seat B2B product early:**
    - Global per-person prices run from $9 (Truity bulk) to $20 (Enneagram) to $25–49 (VIA, Gallup) to $76–90 (DiSC).
    - Offer schools and HR a PKR per-seat price with volume tiers like VIA's ($49 falling to $25), plus a no-minimum team subscription like 16P's.

12. **Use a validated instrument for anything "serious":**
    - Build an IPIP Big Five test (public domain; verify) for employers and universities.
    - Build an O*NET/RIASEC career test (open licence; verify) for schools.
    - Keep 16 types for consumer fun. Follow 16P's terms: do not sell 16 types for hiring decisions.

13. **Sell certification and workshops, the most profitable B2B line:**
    - MBTI certification costs $2,995–3,695, and a UN DiSC workshop costs $1,600 plus per-head fees.
    - Create a "Mizaj Certified Facilitator" course for teachers, HR staff and counsellors, and team-building workshops for Pakistani corporates and universities.

14. **Add recurring revenue only where it fits the value:**
    - A paid newsletter (16P runs a paid Substack), or credits for professionals that never expire (16P Pros).
    - Avoid the weekly-subscription model Boo uses unless you have an ongoing social or matching service.
    - Boo earns about $400K a month [Estimate, 2023] from $5–39.99 subscriptions only because it is a dating app.

15. **Publish credibility signals:**
    - A short technical note on reliability (Truity publishes a manual).
    - A "how we built the model" page (16P's "Our Framework").
    - Clear refund terms.
    - Credibility drives B2B sales and protects against the "MBTI is pseudoscience" criticism.
